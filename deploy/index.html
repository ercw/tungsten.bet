tungsten.bet

Eric Wall bets a tungsten cube against people who are sure. The claim is made in public, the test for changing their mind is their own, the deadline is fixed, and the loser pays in metal. This site keeps the record, and gives each open bet a page where the evidence is laid out against that test so the other side can score it.

I am going to win those bets, and stack those cubes. I am going to build a pyramid of your failed bets vs. me, in tungsten.

A tungsten cube is small and absurdly heavy, and it is not about the money. It is a thing the loser has to buy, have engraved and hand over, and that the winner keeps on the desk. The first one came from Adam Back, with his face on it. The plan, stated in 2023, is a stack of twenty.

How it works

  1. Someone states, in public, that something is obvious. Would bet a lot of money that quantum computing is obvious bullshit. — jamesob, 31 May 2025
  2. Eric offers the bet: a tungsten cube and a deadline. The other side's own test for changing their mind becomes the terms. Let's do a 3 year bet. Tungsten cube? I hold your morals to such a high standard, I don't think we need to specify further. — ercwl, 31 May 2025
  3. A handshake in the thread is the contract. Done 🤝 — jamesob, 31 May 2025
  4. The bet gets a page here: the evidence, laid on the ladder the other side named, with a source and a caveat on every item and the author's ratings marked as such. The page does not score the bet. The other side does.
  5. On the date, the loser hands over a cube. The tradition is that it carries the loser's face. Adam had to pay up a tungsten cube with his face engraved into the cube. — Cointelegraph Magazine, Jan 2023
  6. Anyone can correct the record. The site is a GitHub repository; changes to an item, a rating or this table are taken by pull request when they cite a primary document.
